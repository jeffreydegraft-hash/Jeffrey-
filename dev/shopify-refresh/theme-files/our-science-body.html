We prepare them with care in a base of olive squalane, so that an oil made for prayer, worship, and blessing also nourishes the skin. This page explains what is in every bottle.

Olive-derived squalane: the base of every oil

We formulate our anointing oils in a base of 100% olive-derived squalane, a naturally occurring lipid closely related to the squalene found in human skin.

Squalane is a light, stable oil. It spreads easily, carries the botanical extracts with it, and leaves the skin feeling hydrated rather than greasy. We use no chemical solvents or artificial emulsifiers.

Squalane comes from the fruit of the olive tree, a tree that appears in Scripture more than any other: the oil that lit the lamps of the tabernacle (Exodus 27:20), the base of the holy anointing oil (Exodus 30:22-25), and the oil with which Samuel anointed David (1 Samuel 16:13). Our name comes from the same tree: the Mount of Olives, where Jesus prayed in the garden of Gethsemane.

Polyphenol Alpha (α)™: our proprietary complex

Central to every Mount of Olives formulation is Polyphenol Alpha (α)™, our proprietary antioxidant-rich polyphenol complex, derived entirely from natural botanical sources. It brings together several families of plant polyphenols: flavonoids, phenolic acids, stilbenes, and lignans.

Polyphenol Alpha (α)™ is not a single ingredient. It is a blend of many compounds working together, much the way Scripture describes the body: “many parts, but one body” (1 Corinthians 12:20).

Naturally derived. Scripturally rooted.

Every active compound in our formulations is a natural derivative of a plant, resin, fruit, or seed with deep biblical significance. We do not synthesize our actives in a laboratory. We extract, purify, and concentrate them from the same botanical sources that God placed in creation.

  • Boswellic acids from Boswellia serrata (frankincense), the resin of the Magi
  • Terpenoids and sesquiterpenes from Commiphora myrrha (myrrh), the resin of purification
  • Ellagitannins and punicalagins from Punica granatum (pomegranate), one of the seven blessed fruits of the Promised Land
  • Hydroxytyrosol and oleuropein from Olea europaea (olive), the tree of peace
  • Apigenin and flavonoids from Hyssopus officinalis (hyssop), the herb of purification (Psalm 51:7)
  • Jatamansone and sesquiterpenes from Nardostachys jatamansi (spikenard), the precious oil of Mary of Bethany
  • Labdanum and polyphenols from Cistus ladaniferus (rose of Sharon), the flower of Song of Solomon

These are not synthetic analogs or lab-created substitutes. They are the natural constituents of the very plants Scripture names: extracted, concentrated, and blended into our oils.

Our anthology of ingredients

A reference of the naturally occurring compounds found across our formulations, each one derived from plants, resins, fruits, and seeds with a long history in biblical tradition.

From the olive

Hydroxytyrosol. A polyphenol found mainly in olives and olive oil. An antioxidant.

Tyrosol. A phenolic antioxidant found in olives, olive oil, and wine.

Oleuropein-aglycone dialdehyde. A secoiridoid polyphenol from olives. An antioxidant.

Oleuropein-aglycone mono-aldehyde. A closely related olive secoiridoid, also an antioxidant, that rounds out the olive polyphenols in our formulations.

Ligstroside-aglycone di-aldehyde. A secoiridoid compound found in olives and extra virgin olive oil. An antioxidant.

Pinoresinol. A lignan found in olives, olive oil, and sesame seeds. An antioxidant.

Olive oil (Olea europaea). Cold-pressed from the fruit of the olive tree, the most frequently referenced tree in all of Scripture. Rich in oleic acid, squalene, hydroxytyrosol, and tocopherols. A nourishing emollient and the natural source of the compounds at the heart of our Polyphenol Alpha (α)™ complex.

Squalane (olive-derived). A fully saturated, stable hydrocarbon made by hydrogenating squalene from Olea europaea (olive). Closely related to the squalene naturally present in human skin, it is a light emollient that leaves the skin feeling hydrated, not greasy. Non-comedogenic, odorless, and oxidatively stable: the carrier for our Polyphenol Alpha (α)™ complex.

Frankincense and myrrh

Boswellia serrata (frankincense). A resin from the bark of the Boswellia serrata tree, native to India and the Middle East. It contains boswellic acids (AKBA and KBA). One of the three gifts the Magi presented to the Christ child (Matthew 2:11), and a cornerstone ingredient in ancient anointing formulations.

Commiphora myrrha (myrrh). An oleoresin harvested from the Commiphora myrrha tree of the Arabian Peninsula and northeastern Africa. It contains terpenoids (curzerene, furanoeudesma-1,3-diene), sesquiterpenes, and flavonoids. A gift of the Magi and a biblical symbol of purification, consecration, and healing.

Botanical resins. Natural oleoresins from frankincense, myrrh, and labdanum: complex mixtures of terpenes, sesquiterpenes, and phenolic acids, used in traditional formulations for millennia.

Herbs and flowers

Hyssopus officinalis (hyssop). The biblical herb of purification named in Psalm 51:7: “Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean.” It contains apigenin, rosmarinic acid, and essential oil terpenes.

Nardostachys jatamansi (spikenard). The precious aromatic rhizome Mary of Bethany used to anoint Jesus before his burial (John 12:3). It contains jatamansone, nardosinone, and sesquiterpenes. One of the rarest and most valued botanicals in our collection.

Cistus ladaniferus (rose of Sharon). The labdanum-producing Mediterranean shrub referenced as the rose of Sharon in Song of Solomon 2:1. Rich in polyphenols.

Pomegranate

Punica granatum (pomegranate). One of the seven species blessed in the Torah (Deuteronomy 8:8). The fruit and seed contain ellagitannins, punicalagins A and B, punicalin, gallic acid, and ellagic acid, all polyphenols.

Ellagitannins. Tannins found in pomegranates, raspberries, and walnuts. Antioxidant polyphenols.

The wider polyphenol family

Apigenin. A flavonoid naturally present in parsley (Petroselinum crispum), chamomile (Matricaria chamomilla), celery (Apium graveolens), and hyssop (Hyssopus officinalis). An antioxidant.

Caffeic acid. A hydroxycinnamic acid found in coffee, wine, and many plants. An antioxidant.

Catechin. A flavan-3-ol polyphenol abundant in tea (Camellia sinensis) and many fruits. An antioxidant.

Chlorogenic acid. A phenolic compound found in coffee and stone fruits. An antioxidant.

Epicatechin. A flavanol found in tea and cocoa. An antioxidant.

Ferulic acid. A phenolic compound from rice bran, wheat, and oats. An antioxidant. A key component of our Polyphenol Alpha (α)™ complex.

Gallic acid. A trihydroxybenzoic acid found in grapes, tea leaves, and oak bark. An antioxidant.

Homovanillic acid. A phenolic compound found in vanilla beans and select botanical sources. An antioxidant.

Isoquercitrin. A quercetin glycoside found in apples, onions, and various plants. An antioxidant.

Luteolin. A flavone found in celery, parsley, and thyme. An antioxidant.

Quercetin. One of the most abundant dietary flavonoids, found in apples, onions, berries, and tea. An antioxidant.

Quercitrin (quercetin-3-rhamnoside). A quercetin glycoside found in citrus fruits and berries. An antioxidant.

Rutin. A flavonoid glycoside found in buckwheat, apples, and citrus. An antioxidant.
